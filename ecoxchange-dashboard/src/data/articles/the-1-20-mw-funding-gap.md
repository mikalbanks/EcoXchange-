There is a segment of the U.S. solar market that produces gigawatts of clean
energy, signs long-term contracts with creditworthy utilities, and still
struggles to raise capital efficiently. It is the **1–20 MW range** — too big for
a credit card, too small for a pension fund.

## The numbers

The distributed and community-scale solar segment produced roughly **3.9 GW** of
new capacity in 2024. Yet the institutions that fund solar — infrastructure funds,
tax-equity desks, large yieldcos — are structured around 100 MW-plus utility
projects. Their diligence and legal costs do not shrink for a 5 MW deal, so they
simply skip it.

The result is a financing gap that we estimate at **$300–700 million per year** in
underserved projects: shovel-ready solar that cannot find efficient equity.

## Why the gap exists

- **Fixed transaction costs.** A Reg D private placement costs roughly the same
  whether you raise $2M or $200M. On a small deal, that overhead is fatal.
- **Diligence that does not scale down.** Funds run the same legal and technical
  review on a 5 MW project as a 250 MW one.
- **No standardized product.** Every small raise is bespoke, so capital never
  develops a repeatable pattern for the segment.

## How EcoXchange fills it

EcoXchange standardizes the small-project raise into a single product — the
production-verified Solar Note — and uses tokenization to collapse the fixed
costs:

1. A developer onboards a project and connects production data.
2. The project is backtested and verified by the reconciliation engine.
3. The offering is structured once and opened to accredited investors as ESN
   tokens.
4. Distributions are paid in USDC, automatically, from verified production.

That turns a bespoke, six-month process into a repeatable, weeks-long one — and
makes the 1–20 MW segment investable at the scale it deserves.
