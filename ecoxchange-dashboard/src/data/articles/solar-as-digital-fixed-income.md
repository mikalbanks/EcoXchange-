Investors reach for fixed income when they want predictable cash flows and lower
volatility than equities. Contracted solar infrastructure has a similar shape — a
long-dated stream of payments backed by a creditworthy counterparty — but with
yields that bonds have not offered in years.

## The risk-adjusted case

Operating solar projects with signed PPAs have historically delivered **12–16%
levered IRRs**, with realized volatility roughly **one-third** that of the S&P 500.
The revenue is contracted for 15–25 years and indexed to a physical asset that
keeps producing regardless of market sentiment.

| Asset | Typical annual return | Relative volatility |
|---|---|---|
| S&P 500 | ~10% | High (~17.6%) |
| Investment-grade bonds | 4–5% | Low |
| Contracted solar (levered) | 12–16% | ~⅓ of equities |

## Why the volatility is low

- **Contracted revenue.** A 20-year PPA fixes the price of output, often with an
  annual escalator.
- **Physical backing.** The cash flow is tied to sunlight and hardware, not to
  earnings cycles or rate expectations.
- **Verification.** On EcoXchange specifically, distributions track *verified*
  production, which removes the reporting risk that plagues private deals.

## Where it fits in a portfolio

> Think of production-verified solar as the income sleeve of an alternatives
> allocation: bond-like predictability, equity-like yield, and a low correlation
> to both.

It is not a substitute for liquidity — ESN tokens are long-term holdings with
limited secondary markets. But for the portion of a portfolio earmarked for
durable, real-asset income, contracted solar is one of the more compelling
risk-adjusted options available to accredited investors today.
